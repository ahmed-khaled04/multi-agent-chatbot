# Cash Withdrawals and Direct Debits

Use the transaction tools before making claims about a specific cash withdrawal
or direct debit. Fees, amounts, currencies, and statuses must come from the
customer's transaction record when available.

## Cash withdrawal declined

A withdrawal may be declined because of insufficient available balance, cash
machine limits, card restrictions, an incorrect PIN, network problems, or a
security check. A decline normally does not debit the account.

The customer should avoid repeated attempts when the application shows a
security restriction. Cash-machine owners can impose limits or fees separate
from the bank.

## Cash not received or incorrect amount

If the account was debited but the machine dispensed no cash or the wrong
amount, inspect the withdrawal record. The customer should keep the receipt,
record the machine location and time, and avoid counting or displaying cash in
an unsafe location.

Some failed cash withdrawals reverse automatically within seven calendar days.
If the entry completes with missing or incorrect cash, the customer may open a
dispute after explicitly requesting it. A dispute investigation does not
guarantee reimbursement.

## Withdrawal fees and exchange rates

The transaction record shows bank fees known to the bank. The cash-machine
owner may display and charge an additional fee before confirmation. For a
foreign-currency withdrawal, the machine may offer its own currency conversion.
The customer should review the displayed rate and total before accepting it.

Do not calculate an account-specific exchange result from a historical or
published example. Use the recorded transaction or authorized current-rate
tool.

## Direct debits

A direct debit authorizes a merchant or service provider to collect payment
from an account. Pending direct debits can reduce the available balance before
completion. A merchant name on the statement may differ from the brand the
customer recognizes.

For an unfamiliar direct debit, first inspect the transaction and ask whether
the customer recognizes the merchant or subscription. If it remains
unrecognized, treat it as a security concern and follow the dispute process.

Cancelling a dispute does not cancel a merchant mandate or subscription. The
customer may also need to contact the merchant or use an authenticated account
control to stop future collections.

## Escalation

Escalate when:

- A failed withdrawal has not reversed after seven calendar days.
- The machine dispensed the wrong amount and the transaction completed.
- The cash-machine fee differs from the amount confirmed by the customer.
- A direct debit is unrecognized or continues after merchant cancellation.
